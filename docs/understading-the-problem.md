1.1 Background of the Problem
Choosing a career is an important decision in a person's life. However, many students, recent graduates, and job seekers face difficulties identifying suitable career paths, understanding the skills required for different professions, and finding reliable career guidance. Traditional career guidance services may be limited, difficult to access, or unable to provide enough personalized support.
In addition, information about educational opportunities, job vacancies, professional skills, and career development is often scattered across different websites and social media platforms. As a result, individuals may struggle to make informed career decisions.
The Community-Driven Career Navigation Platform aims to address these challenges by providing a shared online platform where users can explore career opportunities, exchange experiences, receive guidance from others, and access useful career-related information.
1.2 Understanding the Real Environment
The problem exists in educational institutions, universities, online communities, and the employment market. Students and graduates need reliable information to connect their education and skills with suitable career opportunities.
The main target users include:
Students: Need help selecting career paths and understanding future opportunities.
Recent graduates: Need guidance on entering the job market and developing professional skills.
Job seekers: Need information about job opportunities, qualifications, and application processes.
Experienced professionals: Can share knowledge, provide mentorship, and guide less-experienced users.
Educational institutions: Can help students access career information and professional development resources.
In the real environment, users may depend on friends, family members, social media, or personal internet searches for career advice. These sources can be useful, but the information may be incomplete, outdated, or unreliable.
1.3 Root Causes of the Problem
The major root causes include:
Limited access to career guidance: Not all students and job seekers can access professional career counselors or mentors.
Scattered career information: Information about jobs, skills, education, and training is distributed across multiple sources.
Lack of professional networking: Beginners may have difficulty connecting with experienced professionals in their fields of interest.
Unclear career requirements: Users may not understand which skills, qualifications, and experiences are required for specific careers.
Limited peer support: Students and job seekers may lack a dedicated community where they can discuss career challenges and exchange experiences.
Difficulty verifying information: Users may struggle to determine whether career advice and opportunity information are accurate and trustworthy.
1.4 Impact on Target Users
These problems can have several negative effects:
Students may select academic fields or career paths without sufficient information.
Graduates may struggle to identify suitable entry-level jobs or develop the skills employers expect.
Job seekers may waste time searching multiple sources for relevant opportunities.
Beginners may miss opportunities to learn from experienced professionals.
Professionals may have limited opportunities to share their knowledge with people who need guidance.
Educational institutions may find it difficult to connect students with relevant career resources and mentorship opportunities.
These challenges can contribute to uncertainty, inefficient career planning, skill gaps, and difficulties transitioning from education to employment.
1.5 Exact Problem Definition
The exact problem is the lack of an accessible, centralized, and community-driven platform that helps students, recent graduates, and job seekers discover suitable career paths, access relevant career information, connect with experienced professionals, and receive practical guidance for career development.

1.6 Proposed Solution
The proposed platform will provide the following features:

Career exploration: Users can explore different professions and learn about their requirements.
Community discussions: Members can ask questions, share experiences, and exchange career advice.
Mentorship: Users can connect with experienced professionals for guidance.
Career resources: Users can access learning materials, skill-development resources, and relevant opportunity information.
Search and recommendations: Users can find career information and resources related to their interests.
Information moderation: Administrators can review reported content and help maintain a reliable community.
1.7 Conclusion
The Community-Driven Career Navigation Platform is intended to reduce the difficulties people face when searching for career guidance and planning their professional futures. By combining career information, community participation, and mentorship opportunities, the platform aims to help users make better-informed career decisions and prepare more effectively for employment.
The project's effectiveness should be evaluated through user feedback, the accessibility and relevance of career information, participation in the community, and users' ability to identify appropriate next steps in their career development.
